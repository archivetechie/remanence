=== ANALYSIS OF THE OOM ARTIFACT (added 2026-07-30) ===

One of the four processes exited nonzero, writing
fuzz/artifacts/rem_parity_bootstrap_structured/oom-da39a3ee5e6b4b0d3255bfef95601890afd80709
(fuzz/artifacts is gitignored, so it is not in the repository).

It is NOT a defect in the parser. Evidence:

  ==375142== ERROR: libFuzzer: out-of-memory (used: 4097Mb; limit: 4096Mb)
  Live Heap Allocations: 33,262,999 bytes in 31,061 chunks;
    quarantined: 38,476,190 bytes in 371,807 chunks;
    3,073,771 other chunks; total chunks: 3,476,639
  21,233,984 byte(s) (63%) in 1 allocation(s)
      #1 ... /lib/x86_64-linux-gnu/libstdc++.so.6+0xbb903

  1. The artifact is ZERO BYTES — its name is the SHA-1 of the empty string.
     Re-running the target on it executes in 0 ms and exits cleanly, so it
     does not reproduce.
  2. Live heap at the moment of the OOM was ~33 MB against an RSS of 4,097 MB.
     Nothing in the target was holding memory.
  3. The dominant single allocation (63%) arrives through libstdc++
     operator new -- libFuzzer's own C++ machinery -- not through the Rust
     parser under test.
  4. RSS sat at exactly 4,092 MB for many thousands of iterations before
     tipping one megabyte over: slow accumulation across 58 million
     executions in one long-lived process (ASan quarantine plus libFuzzer
     corpus/feature bookkeeping over a corpus that grew past 26,000 files),
     not a per-input blow-up.

The empty input is recorded merely because it is what the process held when
the ceiling was crossed, which is why the reproducer is meaningless.

Consequence for the runner, not for the format: any fixed RSS ceiling is
eventually reached by a long single-process run over a large shared corpus.
The runner's default ceiling was raised to 12 GiB so that a genuine unbounded
allocation in the target is what trips it. A future OOM artifact that is
NON-EMPTY and REPRODUCIBLE is a real finding and must be treated as one.
